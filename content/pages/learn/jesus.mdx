## Jesus in Islam (peace be upon him)

Muslims love and honour Jesus, known in Arabic as ʿĪsā ibn Maryam ("Jesus, son of Mary"). He is one of the greatest messengers God ever sent, and believing in him is part of the Islamic faith itself: a person cannot be a Muslim while rejecting Jesus. The Quran mentions him by name 25 times and gives him special titles found for no other prophet. It calls him the Messiah (al-Masīḥ), "a word from Him" and "a soul [created at a command] from Him" (Quran 3:45, 4:171). Muslims count him among the five greatest messengers, the prophets of "firm resolve": Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus and Muhammad.

### His mother, Mary

Mary holds a place of honour in Islam that few people realise. She is the only woman mentioned by name in the Quran, and she is mentioned more often there than in the entire New Testament. The angels told her: "Indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds" (Quran 3:42).

The Quran tells her story from before her birth. Her mother, from the family of ʿImrān, dedicated her unborn child to God's service. Mary grew up devoted to worship in the temple in Jerusalem, under the care of the prophet Zechariah. A whole chapter of the Quran, Surah Maryam, is named after her, and another, Āl ʿImrān, is named after her family. Muslims see her as a model of faith, purity and patience for all believers, men and women.

### A miraculous birth

The angels brought Mary the good news of "a word from Him, whose name will be the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary – distinguished in this world and the Hereafter" (Quran 3:45). Jesus was born to her without a father, and Muslims believe this completely. Yet a miraculous birth does not make him divine. The Quran explains: "The example of Jesus to Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust; then He said to him, 'Be,' and he was" (Quran 3:59). Adam had neither father nor mother, and no one considers him God.

When Mary brought the baby to her people, they accused her. She pointed to the child, and the newborn Jesus spoke from the cradle to defend her: "Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet" (Quran 19:30). His very first words were that he is God's servant. He also said: "Peace is on me the day I was born and the day I will die and the day I am raised alive" (Quran 19:33).

### His message

Jesus was sent as a messenger to the Children of Israel. He confirmed the Torah that Moses had brought before him, and God gave him a scripture of his own, the Gospel (Injīl), "in which was guidance and light" (Quran 3:49, 5:46). Muslims believe in the original Gospel revealed to Jesus as the word of God.

His message was the same one every prophet brought, from Adam to Muhammad: worship the One God alone. In the Quran he tells his people to "worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord" (Quran 5:117). He called them back to sincere faith, obedience to God's law and good character. When most of his people turned away, a small group of disciples (al-Ḥawāriyyūn) stood with him: "We have believed in Allah and testify that we are Muslims [submitting to Him]" (Quran 3:52). In Islam, a "Muslim" is anyone who submits to God, so Jesus and his true followers were Muslims in this original sense.

### His miracles, by God's permission

God supported Jesus with clear miracles, so that people would know he was truly His messenger. The Quran lists them:

- He formed the shape of a bird from clay, breathed into it, and it became a living bird.
- He healed people born blind and people with leprosy.
- He brought the dead back to life.
- He told people what they had eaten and what they had stored in their homes.

Each time, the Quran adds that it happened "by permission of Allah" (Quran 3:49, 5:110). Jesus never claimed this power as his own. Moses parting the sea and Muhammad receiving the Quran were also miracles from God, and none of them made a prophet divine.

### A human messenger, not God

The Quran is clear that Jesus was a human being and a servant of God: "The Messiah, son of Mary, was not but a messenger; [other] messengers have passed on before him. And his mother was a supporter of truth. They both used to eat food" (Quran 5:75). Someone who needs food to live depends on God, just like every other human being.

The Quran also describes a conversation on the Day of Judgment. God will ask Jesus whether he told people to worship him and his mother. Jesus will answer: "It was not for me to say that to which I have no right… I said not to them except what You commanded me – to worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord" (Quran 5:116–117). Muslims therefore see the belief in Jesus as God, or as the Son of God, as a later addition that Jesus himself never taught. Honouring him truly means following what he actually taught.

### The cross

Muslims believe Jesus was not killed and not crucified. The Quran says: "They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; but [another] was made to resemble him to them… Rather, Allah raised him to Himself" (Quran 4:157–158). God had promised him: "I will take you and raise you to Myself" (Quran 3:55).

So in Islam, the story of Jesus does not end in defeat. God saved His messenger from his enemies and raised him up alive. Muslims also do not believe that anyone needs to die to pay for the sins of others. Each person is responsible for their own deeds, and God forgives whoever sincerely turns back to Him.

### His return

Muslims believe Jesus is alive with God and will return to earth before the Day of Judgment. According to authentic reports from Prophet Muhammad, recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, he will come back as a just ruler. He will defeat the False Messiah (al-Dajjāl), end oppression and fill the earth with justice and peace. He will live a full life, and then die like every other human being.

### Jesus and Muhammad

Jesus and Muhammad, peace be upon them both, are closely linked in Islam. Jesus gave good news of the prophet who would come after him: "a messenger to come after me, whose name is Aḥmad" (Quran 61:6), and Aḥmad is another name of Muhammad. Prophet Muhammad in turn said: "I am the closest of people to Jesus, son of Mary… The prophets are brothers; their mothers are different, but their religion is one" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3442–3443).

This is why Muslims always say "peace be upon him" after the name of Jesus. To follow Islam is to follow the same message Jesus brought: believe in the One God, worship Him alone, and live a life of faith and good deeds.
