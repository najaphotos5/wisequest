## One message, many messengers

Muslims believe that Islam did not begin 1,400 years ago with the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. It is the timeless, universal message conveyed by all prophets throughout human history. From Noah, Abraham, Jacob, Moses, David, John the Baptist, and Jesus, to Muhammad (peace be upon them all), every messenger came with the same fundamental truth: worship the One true God and live righteously by His guidance.

Islam, in this light, is not a new religion but the pure continuation and culmination of God’s message to humanity. It affirms the same core values embraced by sincere believers of the past: faith in God, moral integrity, compassion, and accountability in the hereafter. God is not the author of confusion, and He did not send multiple religions with contradictory beliefs. Divergences emerged over time when people strayed from the authentic teachings of their prophets.

At its heart, *Islam* means submission to the One God alone: recognizing Him as the sole Creator and Sustainer, and following His messengers, not worshiping them.

<Verse cite="Quran 3:85">
Whoever seeks a way other than Islam, it will never be accepted from them, and in the Hereafter they will be among the losers.
</Verse>

Far beyond a set of rituals, Islam is a complete way of life that shapes a believer’s spiritual, ethical, and social conduct. It calls on people to embody justice, mercy, peace, and forgiveness: values that nurture harmony and build bridges across differences.

Sadly, this beautiful message is often obscured by misconceptions and negative portrayals. We invite you to look beyond the stereotypes and explore Islam for yourself.

## What do Muslims believe?

Muslims believe in One, Unique, and Incomparable God; in the Day of Judgement and individual accountability for our actions; and in a chain of prophets beginning with Adam and including Noah, Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Job, Moses, David, Solomon, and Jesus. God’s eternal message was reaffirmed and finalized by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

<Verse cite="Quran 4:163–164 (trans. The Clear Quran)">
Indeed, We have sent revelation to you [O Prophet] as We sent revelation to Noah and the prophets after him. We also sent revelation to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants, [as well as] Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, and Solomon. And to David We gave the Psalms. [There are] messengers whose stories We have told you already and messengers whose stories We have not told you. And to Moses Allah spoke directly.
</Verse>

One becomes a Muslim by sincerely declaring: *“There is no deity but God, and Muhammad is the messenger of God.”* With this declaration, a person affirms faith in **all** of God’s messengers. [Learn how to become Muslim](/become-muslim).

## Does God care about us?

Indeed He does. God created us and did not abandon us. He sent prophets and messengers, from our first father Adam to the final prophet Muhammad, including Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus (peace be upon them all), to guide us and help us make the right choice. He gave us intellect and wants us to use it, and He gave us free will: we are completely free to believe or disbelieve. [Read more in our Q&A](/qa/does-god-care-about-us).

<h2 id="five-pillars">The Five Pillars of Islam</h2>

<Figure src="/img/prayer.jpg" alt="Rows of worshippers bowing together in congregational prayer" caption="Muslims pray side by side, shoulder to shoulder, regardless of race or status." />

1. **Declaration of Faith (Shahada).** Bearing witness that there is no god but God, and that Muhammad is His messenger.
2. **Prayer (Salah).** Five daily prayers: a direct link between the worshiper and God. Islam has no priesthood or hierarchy. [Learn how to pray](/new-muslims/how-to-pray).
3. **Charity (Zakat).** Setting aside a portion of one’s wealth, usually 2.5% of savings each year, for those in need. It “purifies” wealth.
4. **Fasting (Sawm).** Every year in the lunar month of Ramadan, Muslims fast from first light until sunset as an act of devotion and self-purification.
5. **Pilgrimage (Hajj).** A once-in-a-lifetime journey to Mecca for those who are physically and financially able.

## Why it matters

If you were about to travel to a country you had never visited, you would research it: its currency, climate, visa rules. You would plan carefully. How much more does our final, one-way journey deserve? Every one of us will travel, sooner or later, from this very short life to the eternal one.

<Verse cite="Hadith Qudsi, Sahih al-Bukhari 3244 and Sahih Muslim 2824">
Allah the Exalted said: “I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined.”
</Verse>

We make dozens of careful, rational choices every day. The most important choice of all, the one that shapes our eternal life, deserves at least as much thought. The first step is simple: turn sincerely to your Creator and ask Him to guide you.
