---
title: Glossary
description: Common Arabic and Islamic terms, explained simply.
section: New Muslims
---

**Allah**: The Arabic word for God, the One Creator. Arabic-speaking Christians and Jews use the same word.

**Alhamdulillah**: "All praise is for God." Said in gratitude.

**Dua**: Personal supplication, talking to God in your own words, in any language.

**Fitra**: The natural, innate disposition every person is born with to recognize and submit to their Creator. Muslims often describe embracing Islam as "reverting" to this original nature.

**Hadith**: A recorded saying or action of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Collections such as Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim are among the most authentic.

**Hajj**: The pilgrimage to Mecca, required once in a lifetime for those able.

**Insha'Allah**: "If God wills." Said when speaking about the future.

**Masjid**: Mosque, a place of prayer and community.

**PBUH / ﷺ**: "Peace be upon him" / "May God's peace and blessings be upon him." Said after mentioning a prophet; ﷺ is used after the name of Prophet Muhammad.

**Quran**: The final revelation of God, revealed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in Arabic over 23 years.

**Ramadan**: The ninth month of the Islamic calendar, when Muslims fast from dawn to sunset.

**Salah**: The five daily prayers.

**Sawm**: Fasting.

**Shahada**: The testimony of faith: "There is no god but God, and Muhammad is the messenger of God."

**Subhan'Allah**: "Glory be to God." Said in awe or wonder.

**Sunnah**: The way and example of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

**Surah / Ayah**: A chapter / a verse of the Quran.

**Wudu**: The ritual washing before prayer.

**Zakat**: Obligatory annual charity, usually 2.5% of savings above a minimum threshold.
